Word Yard — where the word lists come from, and their terms
===========================================================

Files built from these sources (by build.ts in this folder):
  enable.txt.gz                                      the full list the service checks words against
  everyday.txt.gz                                    everyday words, for the easy computer player
  src/features/arena/games/words/practice-words.ts   the practice board's short list


1. ENABLE — the Enhanced North American Benchmark Lexicon
---------------------------------------------------------
Every word in all three files comes from this list.

Originators: Alan Beale and M. Cooper.
Licence:     public domain.
List:        http://www.puzzlers.org/pub/wordlists/enable1.txt (National Puzzlers' League), as
             kept by the Internet Archive:
             https://web.archive.org/web/20160118193139/http://www.puzzlers.org/pub/wordlists/enable1.txt
             172,823 entries, 1,743,363 bytes,
             sha256 3f16130220645692ed49c7134e24a18504c2ca55b3c012f7290e3e77c63b1a89
Statement:   the list's own readme, published by the same host:
             https://web.archive.org/web/20170312132958/http://www.puzzlers.org/dokuwiki/doku.php?id=solving:wordlists:about:enable_readme
             Both pages were fetched and read on 1 October 2026. The same statement is quoted in
             the Copyright file of SCOWL 2020.12.07 (source 2).

The statement, as written there:

  The ENABLE master word list, WORD.LST, is herewith formally released into the Public Domain.
  Anyone is free to use it or distribute it in any manner they see fit. No fee or registration
  is required for its use nor are "contributions" solicited (if you feel you absolutely must
  contribute something for your own peace of mind, the authors of the ENABLE list ask that you
  make a donation on their behalf to your favorite charity). This word list is our gift to the
  Scrabble(TM) community, as an alternate to "official" word lists. Game designers may feel free
  to incorporate the WORD.LST into their games. Please mention the source and credit us as
  originators of the list. Note that if you, as a game designer, use the WORD.LST in your
  product, you may still copyright and protect your product, but you may *not* legally copyright
  or in any way restrict redistribution of the WORD.LST portion of your product. This *may*
  under law restrict your rights to restrict your users' rights, but that is only fair.

What that asks of this project: mention the source and credit the originators (the how-to-play
and docs/ASSETS-LICENSES.md do), and never claim the list itself as ours.

What was changed: words shorter than 2 or longer than 13 letters are left out (they cannot be
played on a 13 x 13 board), and 147 slurs are removed (blocklist.ts). 160,085 words are shipped.


2. SCOWL — Spell Checker Oriented Word Lists, 2020.12.07
--------------------------------------------------------
Used only to choose WHICH ENABLE words count as everyday: SCOWL sorts English words into sizes
by how widely known they are. everyday.txt.gz keeps the ENABLE words that are in SCOWL size 10;
the practice list keeps sizes 10 and 20, plus every two-letter ENABLE word.

Source:      http://wordlist.aspell.net/ — scowl-2020.12.07.tar.gz from
             https://sourceforge.net/projects/wordlist/files/SCOWL/2020.12.07/
             (files final/english-words.10, .20 and final/american-words.10, .20)

  mentioned below:

    and that both that copyright notice and this permission notice appear in supporting
    documentation. Kevin Atkinson makes no representations about the suitability of this array

The sizes used (10 and 20) draw on these parts of SCOWL, each named in that Copyright file with
its own terms: the Moby Words II lists (placed in the public domain by Grady Ward), Brian Kelk's
"UK English Wordlist with Frequency Classification" (public domain by its author's stated
intention), and, for the spelling variants, the VarCon package (the same permission notice as
above, Copyright 2000-2016 by Kevin Atkinson). The full Copyright file is in the package and
